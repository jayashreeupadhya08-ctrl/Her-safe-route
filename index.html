📖 Field Investigator's Guide & Controls

Welcome to HER SAFE ROUTE: THE SILENT SIGNAL. You are Investigator Nova, working with residents in Aurelia City to address structural safety, transportation, and public infrastructure issues.

1. Core Philosophy: Collective Civic Responsibility

Women's safety is not an individual burden. The game demonstrates that safer communities require thoughtful urban design, reliable public transit, transparent institutions, and active community care—never victim-blaming or expecting individuals to navigate hostile environments alone.

2. Investigation & Evidence Collection

Explore target locations on the city map. Inspect hotspots to gather physical clues, maintenance work orders, and witness statements. Every clue exposes systemic issues.

3. Evidence Board & Deduction

Connect clues on the Evidence Board to form root-cause deductions. Distinguish between systemic solutions (fixing streetlights, reforming complaint processes) and superficial distractions.

4. Route Planning & Upgrades

Compare pedestrian routes for residents. Spend your municipal budget to repair streetlights, install universal curb cuts, update transit displays, and institute community help hubs. Upgrades dynamically transform route conditions on the map!

5. Scoring & Campaign Finale

Each mission awards up to 100 points based on investigation thoroughness, route planning, empathetic responses, quality of improvements, and budget stewardship. Complete all three missions to earn your official Changemaker title!

Lecture 9: Transit Accessibility & Housing Densities

Notes updated Oct 27, 2026 • 21:40

1. Alonso-Muth-Mills Urban Spatial Model

The standard monocentric city model posits that households trade off commuting costs against land prices. When transit networks experience chronic delays or service deficits, the marginal bid rent gradient shifts disproportionately, affecting peripheral wage workers.

2. Pedestrian Catchment Radii

  • Standard transit-oriented development (TOD) assumes an 800-meter (10-minute) walking radius to rail nodes.
  • Micro-accessibility barriers (unramped curbs, unlit crosswalks) effectively reduce pedestrian catchment areas by up to 45% for mobility-restricted populations.
  • First-mile / last-mile safety deficits create artificial transit deserts even within high-density municipal cores.

3. Municipal Capital Allocation Strategies

Public infrastructure investments in active pedestrian corridors yield an estimated 3.8x return in economic vitality and social capital retention over 10-year horizons.
